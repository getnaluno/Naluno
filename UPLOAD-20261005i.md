## Luganda is now read by an African voice
- **No English voice reads Luganda any more.** Luganda goes to an African
  voice: a Kiswahili speaker (a Bantu voice from East/Central Africa,
  Kiswahili TTS Dataset from Lanfrica, Piper voice *sw_CD-lanfrica*).
  Kiswahili shares Luganda's five pure vowels, nasal clusters (mb, nd, ng,
  ny) and syllable shape.
- **It is part of the site.**
  - The voice file is in `voices/af/`, made smaller (63 → 19 MB) so it
    uploads to GitHub.
  - It runs on the phone, so nothing is paid for and no server is asked.
  - A phone downloads it once, the first time it reads Luganda; after that
    it also works offline.
- **It gets Luganda sounds, never spelling.** They are written in the
  symbols this voice was trained on:
  - long vowels as doubled vowels;
  - j as in Kiswahili;
  - the Luganda r/l tap.
- **Doubled letters (bb, kk, ss, nny, jj…)** are doubled and said strongly.
- **No Kiswahili stress pattern is added.** Luganda has tones, not stress.
- **Pace:** a moderate Luganda pace, about 5–6 syllables a second. The old
  voice read Luganda at about 3 a second, which is part of why it sounded
  like a learner.
- **Measured** with a Kiswahili speech recogniser on 17 Luganda sentences
  (the share of letters it heard wrong; lower is clearer):

  | voice | heard wrong |
  |---|---|
  | old English voice, female | 38% |
  | old English voice, male | 41% |
  | **African voice (male)** | **28–34%** |
  | **African voice (female setting)** | **36–39%** |
  | a Southern African (Setswana) female voice, also tried | 59–65% |

- **Female:** there is no open female East African voice. The female
  setting is the same African speaker with the pitch raised (×1.65) and the
  voice made lighter, done on the phone. Listen to
  `luganda-african-female.wav`: if it doesn't sound right to you, I'll make
  both settings use the male voice.
- **Licence (please read):** the Kiswahili recordings may be used "for
  non-profit, educational, and public benefit purposes" (the Lanfrica
  dataset page). If Naluno will charge for anything that uses this voice,
  ask Lanfrica first. The details are in `voices/af/ABOUT.md`.
- **The order is now:**
  1. African voice;
  2. the optional Sunbird voice (only if you ever add a key);
  3. the old voice from Luganda sounds (only if the African voice can't
     load).
- **English** stays with Naluno's English voices. English sentences with
  Luganda names (Kampala, Nakato) keep the names' Luganda sounds.

## Moderation: one rule, and the machine taught what clothes look like
- **One rule, word for word, on the phone and on the server.** The server
  was still using the strict 29h group rule: two or more people plus *any*
  weak sign were held. That is what held the 02 Oct photo of two fully
  clothed people in bright clothes. The server now uses the phone's rule
  (two or more people are held only for a clearly exposed bottom), and a
  test fails if the two files ever differ again.
- **Clothes are not skin.** The detector only knows shapes, so a bright
  shirt over a chest or a dress over a lap could look to it like a breast or
  a bottom. A person tells the difference at once: an exposed part is skin
  and clothing is fabric. Now the phone also measures what lies inside every
  "exposed" box:
  - **Skin** of every shade, from very light to very dark, in any light.
    Pixels too dark to tell count as skin. A black-and-white picture gets no
    allowance.
  - **Fabric:** white, grey, blue, green, purple and yellow clothes, and
    vivid dyed reds and oranges.
- **If less than a fifth of an "exposed" box is skin, the score is halved:**
  - a "breast" or "bottom" on fabric is allowed;
  - "genitals" or "anus" on fabric are still held for a person, never let
    out on this alone;
  - a near-certain finding is never reduced.
- **Real explicit pictures are judged exactly as before, whatever the skin
  colour.**
- The rulebook (in both files, and `MODERATION-RULEBOOK.md`) has a new
  section, *What a clothed picture looks like*, that says this in words.
- Phones that haven't updated yet send no skin figure and get no allowance.

## Luganda: the architecture (05h; 05i puts Naluno's own voice first)
**Every sentence goes through: language → Luganda-trained voice (Naluno's own,
then optional Sunbird) → Luganda pronunciation rules → speech.**
1. **Language, sentence by sentence.** A piece can mix Luganda and English.
   - Each sentence is told apart.
   - An English quote inside Luganda stays English.
   - In an English sentence, Luganda names (Kampala, Nakato, Kiwanuka…)
     keep their Luganda sounds and are never anglicised.
2. **African voice first.**
   - **Naluno's African voice** comes first: on the phone, free (05i).
   - The **optional Sunbird voice** comes next, only if a key is set,
     through Naluno's worker. The worker keeps a copy of every sentence, so
     the same sentence is never paid for twice, and the phone never talks
     to Sunbird directly.
   - These voices know Luganda tones and rhythm, which no rule can give.
3. **If that voice isn't set up or can't be reached,** Naluno's on-device
   voice gets Luganda **sounds**, never the spelling. The English rules
   never see a Luganda word.
   - **A lexicon of about 120 words:** common words, greetings, names,
     places, days, months, and the brief's words chatandika, Katonda and
     ebigambo.
   - **Then the rules:**
     - five pure vowels;
     - length;
     - c/ky = "ch", g before i or y = "j";
     - ny, ŋ / ng';
     - l and r;
     - doubled consonants;
     - no English stress.
   - **New rule, from the published phonology:** a vowel at the end of a word
     is always short. 05g lengthened some word endings by mistake.
     - Fixed, for example: *y'entandikwa*, *omwana*.
     - Also fixed: "ch" in *chatandika* and the vowel after mw.
4. **Numbers** are read in Luganda:
   - emu … kkumi; 12 = kkumi na bbiri; 22 = amakumi abiri mu bbiri;
   - kikumi (100), lukumi (1000);
   - a phone number is read digit by digit.
5. **Pace:** Luganda is read a little slower than English (0.9×), with every
   syllable spoken.

**Permanent test suite:**
- `js/lg-suite.json` holds 44 words, 28 names, 12 sentences and 31 numbers.
- `js/lg-suite.test.cjs` checks, for each one:
  - the exact sounds;
  - that every syllable is spoken;
  - no English vowels or stress;
  - short final vowels;
  - language detection.
- It also checks the whole path, with and without the Luganda voice.
- Every change to the voice system must pass it.

**Native-speaker review:** open **naluno.com/voices/lg-review.html** while
signed in.
- Every item plays in the Luganda voice and in the phone voice. You can
  try every Sunbird Luganda voice there, to pick a male one.
- The reviewer marks each item right or wrong, writes how it should sound,
  and taps **Copy report**.
- Send me the report. Corrections go into the lexicon and the suite
  together.

**What still needs native ears** (marked "waiting for review" in the suite):
- Numbers 1–5 change with the noun in real speech (*abantu babiri*). A
  written figure is read in its counting form (*bbiri*).
- Large compound numbers.
- Tone on the phone voice: Luganda tones aren't written, and only the
  Luganda-trained voice can give them.
